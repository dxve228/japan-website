About Japan

A harmonious blend of ancient heritage and futuristic innovation

和

Where Tradition Meets Tomorrow

Japan, an island nation in East Asia, is a captivating destination where centuries-old traditions coexist with technological innovation. From serene bamboo forests to neon-lit cityscapes, every corner tells a story.

The concept of "wa" (harmony) permeates Japanese society, influencing everything from architecture and art to daily interactions and culinary arts.

Travel Etiquette

Essential dos and don'ts for a respectful visit to Japan

✅ Do

🙇

Bow when greeting

A slight bow shows respect. The deeper the bow, the more respect conveyed.

👟

Remove shoes indoors

Take off shoes when entering homes, temples, and many restaurants. Look for a genkan (entryway).

🎌

Carry a small towel

Many restrooms lack paper towels. A small hand towel (tenugui) is essential.

🗣️

Speak quietly on trains

Keep your phone on silent mode (manner mode) and avoid phone calls on public transport.

🗑️

Carry your trash

Public trash cans are rare. Carry a small bag for your waste until you find one.

💴

Use cash often

Japan is still largely cash-based. Always carry yen, especially at small shops.

❌ Don't

🍜

Don't tip

Tipping is considered rude in Japan. Excellent service is the standard, not a bonus.

🚶

Don't eat while walking

It's considered impolite. Enjoy your food at the shop or find a place to sit.

🔊

Don't blow your nose in public

Step into a restroom or private area. Sniffling is preferred over loud nose-blowing.

🛁

Don't wear swimsuits in onsen

Hot springs are enjoyed naked. Wash thoroughly before entering the bath.

📍

Don't stick chopsticks upright

This resembles a funeral ritual. Rest them on the hashioki (chopstick rest) instead.

🚗

Don't jaywalk

Wait for the green man, even if the road is empty. Japanese pedestrians follow signals.

よくある質問

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Japan expensive to visit? ▼

Japan can be budget-friendly with planning. A day pass for trains costs about ¥600-¥1500. Convenience store meals are ¥300-¥700. Budget travelers can manage ¥5,000-¥8,000/day. The Japan Rail Pass (¥29,650 for 7 days) saves money on bullet trains. Free activities like temple visits, park walks, and shrine exploring abound.

Do I need to speak Japanese? ▼

Not necessarily, but learning basic phrases helps enormously. Tourist areas have English signage, and locals are remarkably helpful. Download Google Translate with the Japanese offline pack — its camera feature reads menus and signs in real-time. Simple greetings like "sumimasen" (excuse me) and "arigatou" (thank you) go a long way.

When is the best time to visit? ▼

Spring (March-May) for cherry blossoms and Autumn (October-November) for fall foliage are the most popular. Winter offers fewer crowds, skiing, and illuminations. Summer has festivals but can be hot and humid. Golden Week (late April-early May) and Obon (mid-August) are peak domestic travel times — book well ahead.

Is Japan safe for tourists? ▼

Japan is one of the safest countries in the world. Crime rates are extremely low, and you can walk safely at any hour. Lost items are frequently returned. Trains run on time to the second. The main concerns are natural disasters — check weather apps during typhoon season (August-October) and be earthquake prepared.

What's the best way to get around? ▼

The JRail Pass is excellent for intercity travel via shinkansen (bullet trains). Within cities, IC cards (Suica/Pasmo) work on all trains and buses. Tokyo's metro is world-class. For rural areas, rent a car. Taxis are clean but expensive. Cycling is popular in flat cities like Kyoto and Osaka.

Can I use credit cards in Japan? ▼

Major hotels, department stores, and chain restaurants accept cards, but many small shops, temples, and local restaurants are cash-only. Always carry yen. 7-Eleven ATMs accept international cards. IC card (Suica/Pasmo) can be loaded with cash and used for trains, vending machines, and convenience stores.
